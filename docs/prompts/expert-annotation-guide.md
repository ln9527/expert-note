# Expert Annotation Guide: Creating High-Value Notes

This guide helps experts create annotations that our AI system can easily transform into powerful "Tacit Knowledge" models.

## The Goal: "Teach the Machine How You Think"

Don't just fix the text. Explain the *mental model* behind the fix.

Our AI looks for 5 types of knowledge. Try to include these in your comments:

### 1. Mental Models & Principles (The "North Star")
*   **What:** Your core philosophy or strategic belief.
*   **Example:** "Always prioritize clarity over cleverness in an introduction."
*   **Tip:** Use words like "Always," "Never," "The goal is," "Fundamentally."

### 2. Diagnostic Reasoning (The "Why")
*   **What:** The causal logic. Why is this specific sentence good or bad?
*   **Example:** "This sentence is weak because the passive voice hides the actor, making the argument feel tentative."
*   **Tip:** Explain the *consequence* of the error.

### 3. Pattern Recognition (The "What")
*   **What:** Identifying a common trope or mistake.
*   **Example:** "This is a classic 'straw man' argument often seen in weak proposals."
*   **Tip:** Name the pattern if you can.

### 4. Actionable Rules (The "How")
*   **What:** Concrete instructions.
*   **Example:** "Delete the first three words. Start with the verb."
*   **Tip:** Be specific and imperative.

### 5. Edge Cases (The "It Depends")
*   **What:** When a rule doesn't apply.
*   **Example:** "Normally we avoid jargon, but for this technical audience, 'API' is acceptable."
*   **Tip:** Use "However," "Except when," "In this specific case."

---

## Example of a "High-Value" Annotation

**Bad Annotation:**
> "Change this."

**Good Annotation:**
> "This sentence is too long and complex. **(Reasoning)** Break it into two. **(Rule)** In technical writing, keeping sentences under 25 words improves retention. **(Principle)**"
