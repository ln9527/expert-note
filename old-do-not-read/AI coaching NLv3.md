**THEORETICAL BACKGROUND AND HYPOTHESES**

Generative AI and Employee Performance: Research Progress and Current Limitations

Studies document substantial performance improvements across various knowledge work domains. In creative tasks, Lee and Chung (2024) found that ChatGPT significantly enhanced human performance in creative problem-solving through its concept integration and remote association capabilities, particularly in incremental innovation tasks. The improvement operated through two key mechanisms: first, ChatGPT served as an "idea elaboration assistant" helping users organize and express concepts; second, this assistance enhanced idea clarity and coherence, leading to significant improvements in creativity scores.

In professional consulting, Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) conducted a large-scale field experiment with management consultants and found that generative AI assistance significantly improved consultant performance within the AI's capability boundaries. Consultants using generative AI completed more tasks, finished tasks faster, and produced higher quality results compared to the control group. In writing tasks, Noy and Zhang (2023) found that ChatGPT substantially reduced completion time while improving output quality in mid-level professional writing tasks.

Efficiency gains extend to technical domains as well. Peng et al. (2023) demonstrated that GitHub Copilot significantly enhanced software developer productivity, with the treatment group completing tasks substantially faster than the control group. In customer service, Brynjolfsson et al. (2025) analyzed millions of customer conversations and found that generative AI tools improved employee efficiency while enhancing customer experience. The study revealed that AI accelerated skill development: AI-assisted employees achieved skill progression levels in months that traditionally required much longer timeframes.

Research has also revealed a fundamental tension in AI-human collaboration: the "efficiency-diversity paradox." While AI enhances individual performance, it may reduce collective diversity and creativity. Doshi and Hauser (2024) found that generative AI significantly improved individual creativity but increased story similarity, suggesting that individual gains in AI-assisted creativity come at the cost of reduced collective content diversity. Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) confirmed this homogenization trend through semantic similarity analysis, identifying convergent patterns in AI-assisted outputs compared to control groups.

This paradox reflects deeper structural characteristics of generative AI systems. As noted by Girotra et al. (2023), generative AI generates results based on statistically most probable text sequences, producing "averaged" outputs based on large training datasets that excel in quality and feasibility but show structural limitations in breakthrough innovation and uniqueness. The tension between efficiency gains and diversity preservation represents a critical challenge for organizations seeking to harness AI's benefits while maintaining innovation ecosystem vitality.

Research demonstrates that AI's performance effects are not uniform across employees, revealing significant "capability convergence effects." Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) found that below-average consultants benefited most from AI assistance, while high-performing consultants showed smaller gains. Doshi and Hauser (2024) observed that AI particularly helped low-creativity authors improve performance, eliminating differences between high and low-creativity authors. This "weak-strengthening" hypothesis challenges traditional technology adoption patterns where high performers typically benefit more, suggesting that generative AI may serve as an equalizing force in workplace performance.

These findings establish that generative AI can enhance workplace performance through multiple pathways: automating routine tasks, providing creative inspiration, offering real-time feedback, and serving as an intelligent collaborator in complex problem-solving activities (Luo et al., 2021; Jia et al., 2023). Importantly, the research reveals that these benefits are not uniformly distributed---they vary significantly across employee characteristics, task types, and organizational contexts, with consistent evidence that lower-performing individuals tend to benefit more from AI assistance than their higher-performing counterparts.

**Research Hypotheses**

Human capital theory posits that strategic investments in employee learning and development generate positive returns through enhanced skills, knowledge, and capabilities that translate into improved job performance (Becker, 1964; Schultz, 1961). Building on this foundation, AI coaching represents a fundamentally new form of human capital investment that transcends the limitations of traditional training approaches. Unlike conventional training programs constrained by standardization and resource limitations, AI coaching offers personalized, adaptive, and continuous learning experiences that are precisely tailored to individual developmental needs.

We argue that AI coaching's performance benefits emerge through four interconnected mechanisms that systematically transform skill acquisition into measurable work performance gains. These mechanisms work synergistically to create a comprehensive learning environment that addresses the key barriers that have historically limited training effectiveness in organizational settings.

Traditional training programs often adopt a one-size-fits-all approach that fails to account for individual differences in baseline competencies, learning styles, and developmental needs (Noe, 1986; Colquitt et al., 2000). This misalignment between training content and individual needs frequently results in inefficient learning and limited performance improvements. AI coaching fundamentally addresses this personalization challenge by dynamically adapting to each employee's specific skill gaps, learning pace, and knowledge level. The system can identify precisely where each employee needs improvement and deliver targeted interventions that maximize learning efficiency. This personalized approach directly enhances work performance by ensuring that employees receive training precisely aligned with their job-specific deficiencies, leading to immediate improvements in task execution quality, problem-solving capabilities, and decision-making speed (Arthur et al., 2003; Kraiger et al., 1993). When employees receive training that directly addresses their performance bottlenecks, the translation from learning to improved work outcomes becomes both immediate and measurable.

Unlike traditional training that occurs in discrete episodes separated by long intervals, AI coaching enables continuous skill development through on-demand access to learning resources and real-time feedback mechanisms (Tannenbaum & Yukl, 1992). This continuous learning approach aligns with modern understanding of skill acquisition as an iterative process requiring sustained practice and feedback (Ericsson et al., 1993). The immediate availability of guidance and correction mechanisms translates directly into performance improvements as employees can quickly address emerging challenges, reduce error rates, and maintain consistently high-quality output without waiting for scheduled training interventions (Ford et al., 2018). This real-time support system particularly enhances critical performance metrics including customer satisfaction, task completion rates, and error reduction, as employees can course-correct their actions in the moment rather than perpetuating suboptimal practices.

One of the most persistent challenges in organizational training is the gap between learning content and actual job performance---the so-called "transfer problem" (Baldwin & Ford, 1988). AI coaching addresses this challenge by facilitating experiential learning through scenario-based practice that allows employees to apply new skills in simulated environments before transferring them to real work contexts. By providing safe spaces for experimentation and mistake-making, AI coaching reduces the psychological barriers to skill application that often limit training effectiveness. This enhanced transfer of learning directly improves job performance by increasing employees' confidence and competence in handling real-world situations, leading to better customer interactions, more effective problem resolution, and increased ability to handle complex or unexpected scenarios (Bell & Kozlowski, 2008). The practice-to-performance pipeline becomes more efficient, with employees demonstrating measurable improvements in performance indicators such as sales conversion rates, customer service quality scores, and operational efficiency metrics.

Through these three interconnected mechanisms, AI coaching creates a comprehensive learning ecosystem that systematically transforms individual skill development into measurable performance improvements, making it a particularly effective form of human capital investment for organizations seeking to enhance employee capabilities and work outcomes.

***Hypothesis 1: AI coaching is positively related to employee performance.***

This hypothesis builds on human capital theory's central insight that learning investments yield higher returns when they address significant skill deficiencies rather than merely refining already-developed competencies (Schultz, 1961; Mincer, 1974). The differential effects of AI coaching across employee groups reflect fundamental differences in baseline competencies, skill gaps, and the nature of work performed by sales versus non-sales employees.

Non-sales employees in customer-facing organizations often lack the interpersonal communication skills, customer interaction expertise, and sales-specific knowledge that are central to effective customer service and relationship building. These employees typically enter their roles with technical or functional expertise in their primary domain (e.g., engineering, accounting, operations) but limited experience in the soft skills required for customer interaction (Pfeffer, 1994). For this population, AI coaching represents a substantial human capital investment that can bridge fundamental skill deficiencies in areas such as communication techniques, emotional intelligence, conflict resolution, and customer relationship management.

The magnitude of potential improvement for non-sales employees is amplified by the skill gap differential. When baseline competencies are low, even modest improvements can yield significant performance gains, a phenomenon consistent with the diminishing returns characteristic of human capital investments (Becker, 1994). Non-sales employees starting from a lower skill foundation in customer interaction have greater room for improvement and thus higher potential returns from AI coaching interventions.

Furthermore, the structured, knowledge-based nature of many customer interaction skills makes them particularly amenable to AI-mediated learning. Skills such as product knowledge dissemination, standard operating procedures, and basic communication protocols can be effectively taught through the scenario-based practice and real-time feedback that AI coaching provides. The systematic approach of AI coaching may be especially beneficial for employees who lack intuitive understanding of customer interaction dynamics but can learn these skills through structured practice and guidance.

In contrast, sales employees typically possess well-developed interpersonal skills, customer interaction expertise, and sales-specific knowledge accumulated through specialized training and extensive experience. Their professional identity and career success depend on their ability to build relationships, understand customer needs, and persuade effectively---skills that represent core competencies rather than developmental areas (Weitz et al., 1986).

For sales employees, AI coaching encounters several theoretical limitations. First, the marginal returns to training are typically lower for individuals who already possess strong baseline competencies (Mincer, 1974). Additional training investments yield diminishing returns as skill levels approach expert performance, making it increasingly difficult to generate substantial performance improvements through conventional learning interventions.

Second, sales work's relational and intuitive nature may limit AI coaching's effectiveness compared to more structured, procedural tasks. Successful sales performance often depends on reading subtle social cues, adapting communication styles to individual customers, and building authentic emotional connections---capabilities that require human judgment and emotional intelligence that are difficult to systematically teach or replicate (Plouffe et al., 2013). While AI coaching can provide structured knowledge and standard techniques, the contextual sensitivity and improvisational skills that characterize expert sales performance may be less amenable to AI-mediated learning.

Third, sales employees may experience expertise-based resistance to AI guidance, preferring to rely on their proven methods and experience rather than adopting new approaches suggested by AI. Research on expert learning suggests that highly skilled individuals may be less receptive to external guidance when it conflicts with their established practices and professional intuition (Dane, 2010). This resistance can limit the effectiveness of AI coaching interventions among employees who already consider themselves competent in the targeted skill areas.

Therefore, we hypothesize that AI coaching will generate stronger performance improvements for non-sales employees, who possess greater skill gaps and face fewer barriers to learning, compared to sales employees, who already possess well-developed competencies and may be less receptive to AI-mediated skill development.

***Hypothesis 2: The positive effects of AI coaching on employee performance are stronger for non-sales employees compared to sales employees.***

**METHODS**

**Data and Samples**

We examined our hypotheses using a dataset from PaPiRa, a prominent medical beauty enterprise in China known for its high-end, customized services. Operating in a competitive market, PaPiRa emphasizes customer satisfaction and employee development, employing around 200 staff members, including sales personnel, beauty technicians, administrative staff, and other support roles. The workforce has diverse experiences and skills, contributing to a dynamic organizational environment.

In November 2023, PaPiRa's management implemented an AI coaching tool, named Lianlian, to enhance service quality and sales performance. This AI-driven system, accessible to all employees regardless of their primary roles, provided personalized, scenario-based training by simulating customer interactions. The AI tool engaged employees in real-time conversations, acting as customers with specific service needs, allowing employees to practice and refine their sales skills. This approach offered a flexible and context-specific alternative to traditional training methods, supporting continuous improvement at individual and organizational levels.

The dataset for this study consists of three primary components: sales performance data, AI coaching tool usage data, and individual characteristics data. Sales performance data was collected over six months from August 2023 to January 2024. Employees at PaPiRa sell three types of beauty cards, priced at 1,980 RMB, 3,980 RMB, and 4,980 RMB. The main measure of sales performance is the total number of beauty cards sold by each employee per month (*Number of Cards*). We also calculated total sales revenue (*Total Sales*) to account for the varying price points of the cards.

AI coaching tool usage data included details on which employees used the tool, as well as specifics on the nature of their usage. To assess the AI coaching tool's impact on sales performance, 200 employees were randomly assigned to either a control group (63 employees) or a treatment group (137 employees). The groups were not strictly balanced due to operational considerations, such as varying levels of employee availability and differing participation rates across departments. During the observation period, the control group did not have access to the AI tool, while the treatment group began using the tool in November 2023, with the freedom to engage in sales script training. Detailed usage records, including scenarios, frequency, and duration of AI interactions, were collected. Individual characteristics data, sourced from PaPiRa's HR, included demographic and work-related information such as age, education level, generative AI usage frequency, industry experience, tenure within the organization, and job roles. After merging and cleaning the data to remove missing values, the final dataset comprised 1,166 employee-month observations from August 2023 to January 2024.

This study uses the quasi-natural experimental setting created by the introduction of the AI coaching tool at PaPiRa to estimate the causal effect of the tool on employees' sales performance. We employed a Difference-in-Differences (DID) approach, which compares changes in sales performance between the treatment and control groups over time, isolating the impact of AI intervention. **STUDY 2: QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS**

To deepen our understanding of how AI coaching influences employee performance and to explore the underlying mechanisms revealed by our quantitative findings, we conducted a complementary qualitative study. This approach uncovers the "black box" processes through which AI coaching operates and explains why different employee groups experience varying benefits.

**Data Collection and Analysis**

To explore how AI coaching shapes employee performance and development, we conducted 30 in-person, semi-structured interviews with a purposive sample of sales employees and non-sales employees, each lasting 20--40 minutes. An interview guide ensured coverage of key topics, including the features of AI coach, perceived benefits and impacts, learning strategies, and attitude changes. We also encouraged interviewees to speak freely, share detailed examples, and express emotions and interpretations (Weiss 1994).

We analyzed the data using a grounded theory approach (Glaser & Strauss, 1967; Strauss and Corbin 1994) with three iterative stages. In the first stage, we examined transcripts line-by-line to identify initial concepts, labeling segments that reflected how employees perceived, used and benefited from AI coaching. Many first-order codes captured employees' curiosity, exploration and instrumental expectations when first using the AI coach. As the data analysis progressed, we grouped these codes into tentative categories, retaining those that recurred across multiple interviews and revising or discarding those that lacked sufficient support. In the second stage, we organized first-order codes into second-order theoretical categories, focusing on contrasts and relationships among the categories (Gioia, Corley, and Hamilton 2013). We generated aggregated theoretical dimensions in the third stage based on second-order categories.

Data analysis revealed four main findings: "AI coaching changes training paradigm," "employee growth model in AI coaching," "the differentiated mechanisms of AI coaching" and "the differentiated outcomes of AI coaching". After discovering these dominant themes, we returned to the data, recoded them on developing categories within the themes and built the model.

**Finding 1: AI Coaching Changes Training Paradigm**

All interviewees emphasized that AI coaching represents a fundamentally new training paradigm, one that departs radically from traditional, instructor-led methods and aligns more closely with the dynamic realities of customer-facing work. Whereas conventional training often involves passive listening to lectures and rote memorization, the AI coach---"Lianlian"---offers an active, responsive, and learner-driven experience that employees uniformly described as transformative.

Our thematic coding revealed three core features underlying this transformation (as shown in Table 1). These features---intelligent simulation practice, adaptive learning, and structured feedback---collectively reshape how employees engage with learning content, acquire skills, and develop professional confidence.

**Feature 1: Intelligent Simulation Practice**

The first key feature of AI coaching is intelligent simulation practice, which enables learners to immerse in realistic customer interactions, moving beyond passive knowledge transfer to active skill development. Employees highlighted four key aspects of this feature. First, interactive practice allows learners to engage in verbal, scenario-based simulations as a transformative experience that helped them internalize rather than memorize content. This illustrates the shift from receptive learning to active verbal rehearsal, an approach that deepens cognitive processing:

"In the past, training sessions involved many instructors and professionals lecturing us. What we hear in class is completely different from practicing and sharing out loud ourselves. 'Lianlian' allows us to simulate and practice conversations, which is really great." (#S3)

"Practicing by simulating interactions with customers and speaking out loud is more effective than rote memorization." (#N9)

Several interviewees also emphasized the scenario variety, which enables them to be exposed to a broad spectrum of customer profiles and service situations. Randomized voices, personas, and problem types prevent habituation, ensuring each session feels fresh and closely mirrors real-world unpredictability:

"The question bank includes all the potential customer issues we might encounter in daily work." (#S6)

"We can practice various scenarios, including pre-service, during-service, and post-service situations, as well as different professional project service scenarios\... Each simulated customer's voice, personality, and other aspects are also different." (#N9)

Another important element of this feature is gamification, which transforms training into an engaging, immersive challenge that sustains motivation and encourages repeated engagement. Employees mentioned that they treat the practicing process "like a game" (e.g., #S5, #N12). One participant also remarked that "You can try joking with it, and it will respond in an interesting way too." (#N14).

Finally, mastery through repetition enables employees to accelerated skill acquisition via deliberate practice. This repetition is not mechanical but iterative---employees adjust, improve, and eventually gain fluency through accumulation and reflection. This iterative loop nurtures a growth mindset, as employees witness firsthand how incremental adjustments yield measurable improvements:

"In the past, instructor-led training did teach us a lot of knowledge. But since what we learn needs to be communicated and shared with customers, if we don't practice repeatedly in daily work, I wouldn't know how to express it for the best results. "There's a lot we need to learn, and some knowledge is forgotten after studying. With 'Lianlian', if there's unfamiliar knowledge, we can practice repeatedly, and it will naturally leave a deep impression in our minds." (#N21)

**Feature 2: Adaptive Learning**

The second feature of AI coaching, adaptive learning, gives employees control over their own development through four principal aspects. Unlike traditional one-size-fits-all training models, AI coaching allows learners to tailor the content and pacing to each their own needs and schedule. The adaptability also supports reflection and self-directed goal-setting. This aligns with a broader shift from prescriptive training to learner agency.

"If I'm not very familiar with a certain project, I'll focus on practicing it more times. For projects I'm more familiar with, I might only practice once." (#N5)

"Our brand covers a wide range of services. Before practicing, I reflect on which professional area I can improve in recently, or I'll specifically choose scenarios where I want to increase customer order value to practice." (#S6)

Fragmented learning is another important aspect. Training can happen in short bursts during downtime, making it more compatible with real work rhythms. Participants described this flexibility as essential to building consistent learning habits:

"Using 'Lianlian' for learning offers flexible scheduling---I can practice whenever I want. Since our free time varies depending on customers' schedules, we can just open the app and practice when there are no clients." (#S3)

"With 'Lianlian', we can practice anytime, anywhere. Even if I only remember 1% today, practicing tomorrow and the day after will help me accumulate more and more." (#N9)

Employees also engage in on-demand exploration by formulating precise queries to solve specific challenges. AI's ability to respond precisely to custom inputs enabled a more exploratory, problem-solving approach to learning:

"When I don't know how to better communicate with customers about a certain project, I proactively turn to 'Lianlian' for methods." (#S5)

"It can provide precise answers based on the commands you input." (#N14)

Crucially, the pressure-free environment of AI coaching fosters risk-taking and builds speaking confidence. This sense of emotional safety catalyzes faster skill development and greater willingness to practice challenging scenarios.

"It won't outright reject us like real customers would; it still gives us plenty of opportunities to communicate." (#S8)

"When interacting with 'Lianlian', I feel I can be more open and speak freely because even if I make a mistake, it's okay---I can just practice again." (#N12)

**Feature 3: Structured Feedback**

The third feature, structured feedback, transforms the learning loop into a data-informed cycle of continuous improvement. Learners receive multidimensional feedback, including quantitative scores and qualitative advice. Participants repeatedly emphasized the value of receiving immediate and precise assessments after each interaction:

"After each session, AI gives a score based on a comprehensive assessment, showing us how to achieve the most comfortable state in customer conversations." (#S2)

"After each practice session, I can review AI's suggestions. It will tell me which aspects need improvement, such as speaking pace, tone, or phrasing. Sometimes when my practice score isn't high, I check AI's evaluation to find the reason---it explains in detail, like advising me to avoid certain filler words." (#N1)

In addition, AI-generated reference answers served as professional-grade models that helped participants benchmark their own performance. Rather than merely pointing out what went wrong, the system showed what good practice looks like, offering a vision of excellence that learners could strive toward:

"We can refine our phrasing based on AI's reference answers, helping us understand the standard for customer interactions." (#S8)

"The best part, I think, is that 'Lianlian' provides suggested answers. As a newcomer, there's a lot I don't know, and its answers serve as great templates for quick learning." (#N15)

Finally, performance tracking allows employees to monitor their progress over time, reinforcing motivation and guiding new goals. In this way, feedback was not just evaluative but developmental, playing a formative role in learners' ongoing growth:

"Through continuous practice, I can see my own improvement. For example, when I first started training in a certain scenario, 'Lianlian' would ask a question, and I'd have to think carefully before responding. But after practicing more, I can reply almost instantly without much thought---my reaction speed has clearly improved." (#S8)

"The score fluctuates based on my verbal expression, including emotional delivery and tone. I also judge the effectiveness of my practice based on score changes." (#N1)

Together, these three features---intelligent simulation practice, adaptive learning, and structured feedback---constitute a cohesive, high-impact training paradigm that all interviewees agreed had fundamentally changed how they learn and perform in customer-facing roles.

**Finding 2: Employee Growth Model in AI Coaching**

Building on these transformative characteristics of AI coaching, we found that employees' experience with the platform unfolds in a clear, stepwise progression from novice to expert. This evolution reveals how the active, learner-driven nature of AI coaching fosters a fundamentally different growth trajectory compared to traditional training methods. We found that employees' experience with AI coaching unfolds in a clear, stepwise progression from novice to expert. To conceptualize this journey, we drew on Dreyfus's (1986) Five-Stage Model of Adult Skill Acquisition, mapping interviewees' descriptions onto five stages---novice, advanced beginner, competence, proficiency, and expert---each characterized by distinctive cognitive orientations and learning strategies. Below we narrate this evolving process, illustrating how employees' mindsets and approaches deepen as they gain familiarity with "Lianlian."

**Stage 1: Novice**

At the novice stage, employees approach the AI coach with an exploratory yet instrumental mindset. Employees engage in function exploration, tentatively probing features and capabilities without clear expectations. Many described initial confusion about the tool's purpose:

"At first, I didn't understand why this was introduced or what it was for." (#N2)

"When we first encountered it, our initial reaction was curiosity and confusion. We approached it with an exploratory mindset, finding it quite fun." (#S8)

Cognitively, they hold an instrumental expectation, viewing the AI coach primarily as a knowledge repository---a searchable database or engine that delivers instant facts:

"At first, I considered it as a knowledge base for us. Whatever you need, it can provide relevant knowledge." (#N2, #N19)

"At first, we treated it as a search engine." (#N3)

In addition, at this stage, employees' learning strategies are opportunistic but superficial. On the one hand, they use on-demand search and turn to the AI coach only when a specific gap emerges:

"Initially, I only used it when I had specific needs and didn't practice daily." (#S8)

"For example, if a customer asks something I can't answer, I'll check 'Lianlian' to see if it can help." (#N3)

On the other hand, they adopt a task-driven focus, practicing narrowly on scenarios related to their immediate tasks without broader reflection:

"At first, I only practiced projects relevant to my current work." (#S1)

"I generally only choose what I need. If it's not work-related, I rarely practice it." (#N17)

**Stage 2: Advanced Beginner**

As they move into the advanced beginner stage, employees rely on the AI coach to solve problems and become preoccupied with performance metrics. They develop noticeable reliance on "Lianlian", instinctively turning to it when uncertain:

"Whenever I encounter something I don't know or understand, I immediately think of asking AI." (#N1)

"There's a sense of dependency---when faced with a question I can't answer, I consider searching on 'Lianlian' to see how it responds." (#N14)

This reliance coincides with score obsession---an intense focus on practice metrics. They believe high scores and rankings represent good learning results:

"After each practice session, I check my score and ranking information." (#N5)

"Seeing others on the leaderboard with higher scores makes me want to surpass them. Since daily statistics reset at midnight, sometimes I'll wait until the last minute, nervously checking if my score has been overtaken." (#S5)

In response, employees' learning strategy features benchmark understanding. They directly copy reference answers and templates, believing that scripts provided by AI represent the gold standard:

"For newcomers or junior employees, the reference answers provide clear standards." (#N18)

"We can refine our phrasing based on AI's reference answers, understanding the benchmark for customer interactions." (#S8)

To internalize these benchmarks, they engage in rote memorization through repetition and imitation. While this produces quick gains in accuracy, it does little to foster adaptive expertise or contextual judgment.

"I used to mainly memorize the standard answers." (#S1)

"The reference answers cover essential knowledge. By practicing and repeating them, we can master the content." (#N18)

**Stage 3: Competence**

Upon reaching competence, employees begin to see the AI coach as a collaborative partner rather than a mere tool. They appreciate how properly guided AI interactions enhance efficiency and fill conversational gaps:

"Using AI properly can make our work more efficient and help us better manage our time." (#S8)

"It helps me optimize details in customer interactions. For example, when my communication with customers tends to be vague, it fills in the gaps and supplements what I might have missed." (#N12)

They also leverage AI to conduct self-assessment, identify strengths and limitations:

"Through deeper usage, I've realized 'Lianlian' can scientifically and accurately guide us in evaluating our own abilities." (#S8)

"I feel it has helped me gain a comprehensive understanding of myself." (#N1)

Correspondingly, their learning strategy evolves into feedback evaluation. They systematically review feedback to pinpoint weaknesses and set targeted improvement goals:

"I review its feedback to identify my weaknesses and strengths. Over time, I notice more areas of improvement." (#S6)

"If you carefully read the analysis and evaluations provided after each practice, you'll unconsciously spot your own detailed issues, recognize shortcomings, and improve." (#N1)

Alongside this analytical approach, they adopt reflective learning, observing peers' practices and experimenting with alternative techniques:

"I found that just reading reference answers might seem clear at the time, but I'd forget after a few days. Now, I practice speaking out loud---the effect is entirely different from just reading." (#S1)

"When I see colleagues performing well in practice, I ask about their methods and reflect on whether I missed certain details." (#N8)

**Stage 4: Proficiency**

In the proficiency stage, employees recognize both the strengths and limitations of AI. They develop critical thinking:

"I use it selectively, focusing on certain aspects. For professional knowledge, it's excellent. But in customer communication---since most clients are women and require more emotional topics---AI can't handle that." (#S3)

"The biggest issue with AI is its over-reliance on templates. Each customer service scenario is unique and can't be fully replicated---responses must be tailored to specific needs and problems." (#N20)

This critical stance leads them to deep integration, blending AI outputs into personalized frameworks rather than following them blindly:

"The ultimate goal of practicing with AI is to seamlessly integrate what we've learned into real customer interactions, rather than relying on rigid theoretical knowledge." (#S8)

"AI learning shouldn't stay superficial. It requires multifaceted, in-depth consideration because every customer is different." (#N16)

Their learning strategy at this stage emphasizes creative usage. The suggestions offered by AI coach serve as springboards for innovation. Employees inject personal anecdotes or team best practices into AI-generated scripts:

"As I practice more, I incorporate more elements, including real-life experiences from myself and colleagues." (#S6)

"Since AI's reference answers are too mechanical and lack warmth, when I communicate with a customer, I use AI for direction---to identify discussion points---but I personalize the actual communication. I think this is the best way to use AI." (#N14)

Employees at this stage also emphasize knowledge transfer, flexibly adapting core expertise and principles to diverse contexts. This stage yields genuine adaptability, as employees fluidly move between AI guidance and spontaneous, context-sensitive responses.

"If a customer's question resembles something I've practiced in 'Lianlian', I can adapt and respond quickly." (#S6)

"Every customer's situation and personality differ---you can't use identical approaches. The core knowledge is similar, but I reflect on how to extract the most concise expertise and adapt the rest." (#N20)

**Stage 5: Expert**

Finally, at the expert stage, employees engage in strategic optimization, taking a meta-level perspective on the AI coach and themselves. They actively steer AI's reasoning processes, asking targeted questions to refine both prompts and practice flows:

"During practice, I constantly ask it: 'Which aspect are you considering---effectiveness, safety, or something else?' I think this is crucial. You need to guide AI to follow your logic to practice what you truly need." (#S4)

"I guide AI toward the outcomes I want. I believe in boldly steering the conversation during practice to align with your goals---the same approach applies with customers." (#S8)

They also embrace boundary challenge, deliberately tackling the most difficult scenarios in the existing training materials---such as complex sales modules---to expand both their own and the AI's capabilities:

"Colleagues often discuss how selling cards in 'Lianlian' is very difficult. I became obsessed with challenging this scenario. Later, I realized that once I understood its mechanics, success came easily." (#S4)

"The card-selling module is the most challenging. We simulate conversations with new customers, engaging them in detailed chats until the sale is successful." (#N16)

Expert learners then shift their strategy to knowledge co-creation, partnering with AI coach to enrich its training database with real-world cases and nuanced scenarios:

"The training materials need continuous iteration. Some questions raised by customers in real practice haven't been covered, leaving us unsure how to respond. After discussing with colleagues and supervisors, we will add them to the training database." (#N20)

"The current database is relatively limited. Gradually, we'll expand it with new, complex projects based on business needs." (#S8)

They also achieve structured understanding, constructing conceptual frameworks that systematically link knowledge domains:

"We need to establish connections between foundational knowledge---like the relationship between gynecology and endocrinology---and systematically link them. 'Lianlian' is a great tool for this." (#N18)

"Through post-practice summaries, I can acquire management-level knowledge and skills, achieving systematic improvement." (#N21)

This five-stage employee growth model in AI coaching illustrates how cognitive orientation evolves from functional exploration to strategic mastery, while learning strategies progress from ad-hoc retrieval to co-creation and structured understanding. The model demonstrates how sustained engagement with AI coaching transforms employees into reflective experts who not only leverage AI for systematic personal gains but also contribute to its ongoing improvement.

**Finding 3: Differentiated Mechanisms of AI Coaching**

Building on the employee growth model and the transformative characteristics of AI coaching outlined above, we turn to dissect the differentiated mechanisms through which sales employees and non-sales employees benefit from AI coaching. This analysis aims to deepen our understanding of how AI coaching's active, learner-driven nature creates distinct pathways for different employee groups, ultimately clarifying the underlying mechanisms of AI coaching effectiveness.

**Mechanism 1: Cognitive support**

Cognitive support refers to the way AI coaching enhances employees' mental processes. It offers employees opportunities to rehearse, refine, and reorganize their thoughts in ways that are difficult to achieve through traditional training. Both sales employees and non-sales employees reported improvements in reasoning, expression, and domain mastery, yet the focus and depth differed.

In terms of rhetoric and logical thinking, sales employees leverage AI for advanced dialogue optimization using it to craft highly personalized, structured scripts that blend multiple templates and refine complex, scenario-specific phrasing:

"Before practice, I might just say whatever came to mind without good logical structure. AI provides professional suggestions that give me a very clear line of thinking. This has significantly improved me." (#S3)

"I learn valuable points from AI's answers and analyses, extract them into my own understanding, and then apply them in my work." (#S5)

In contrast, non-sales employees rely on standardized script training. They mimic AI-provided templates for opening and recommendation dialogues, repeatedly practice these standardized structures to boost accuracy and consistency, and quickly upskill by modeling industry-best phrasing. They focus on establishing a consistent and professional baseline before branching into more adaptive use.

"AI also provides templates for reference. When practicing, what we say ourselves might not be as professional or comprehensive." (#N2)

"Sometimes when you don't know how to respond, you can look at the templates in 'Lianlian' and then apply them in practice." (#N10)

In terms of communication and verbal expression, sales employees demonstrate precise scenario adaptation. They tailor their language to various customers and situations. They mainly leverage the AI coach to deepen and diversify advanced communication skills

"Now I can communicate precisely according to customers' questions. Before, my responses would sometimes go off-topic." (#S1)

"My customer communication has become more detailed, more precise, more thoughtful, and more meticulous." (#S5)

Non-sales employees, by contrast, benefit from communication fundamentals training. They draw primarily on its ability to scaffold foundational language and logic. AI coach provides multidimensional and precise feedback, helping them identify weaknesses in tone, pace, word choice, etc., and transform from colloquial expressions to professional language.

"AI analyzes your language and gives a summary. You can identify your problems and improve accordingly." (#N6)

"We used to speak more colloquially. AI helps us learn more professional expressions. We also practice speaking pace---sometimes we naturally speak too fast. AI has good standards for appropriate speed." (#N8)

In terms of knowledge and business understanding, AI coach helps sales employees achieve expertise pragmatic transfer by deepening their grasp of product and service value propositions and mapping these exactly onto customer needs.

"AI helps me quickly understand the features and selling points of projects and how to apply them in building emotional connections with customers---I think this is excellent." (#S5)

"The ultimate goal of practicing with AI is to seamlessly integrate what we've learned into actual customer communication, rather than just using rigid theoretical knowledge." (#S8)

Non-sales employees, however, benefit mainly from expertise depth expansion. Since they have traditionally prioritized technical skills, they compensate for previously shallow professional knowledge by absorbing the comprehensive and structured explanations provided by AI coaching.

"Actually, I didn't know much about the professional knowledge involved in many company projects---sometimes my explanations felt vague. But through practice, I've gained clarity." (#N6)

"As beauticians, we previously practiced technical skills more and were somewhat lacking in professional knowledge---consultants probably knew more about those project specifics than we did." (#N22)

**Mechanism 2: Motivational empowerment**

Motivational empowerment captures the psychological processes through which AI coaching boosts employees' confidence, engagement, and drive for improvement. Unlike conventional training, AI provides personalized, non-judgmental, and repeatable practice environments that allow employees to experience success incrementally. We found that this mechanism is especially critical for non-sales employees with lower initial confidence or fewer opportunities for traditional success, as AI coaching transforms practice into an empowering, motivating experience.

In terms of self-efficacy and confidence, sales employees experience confidence reinforcement through mastery of high-challenge scenarios, which strengthens their sense of control and resilience to rejection.

"Before serving customers, I can sharpen my skills in advance, which gives me more confidence and makes me more natural when sharing with customers." (#S1)

"Today's customers are very rational and conservative with spending. In such situations, through AI simulation practice, when facing cold rejections from customers, I don't take it personally because the practice constantly exposes me to rejection scenarios." (#S8)

Non-sales employees, on the other hand, benefit from self-efficacy establishment. They have traditionally concentrated on technical procedures and treatment protocols, and frequently felt unprepared to explain service offerings or recommend complementary treatments according to customers' needs. AI coaching changed this dynamic by providing structured, scenario-based learning modules that cover each business project in depth---from product features and indications to persuasive communication strategies---enabling them to build a comprehensive understanding of service portfolios and to confidently introduce and recommend treatments to customers.

"I think the biggest change is having more confidence to introduce and recommend projects to customers." (#N7)

"Before, I didn't like talking much with customers. Even when I wanted to sell a product, I felt awkward communicating. But now through practice, my communication skills have improved, so I take more initiative in discussing projects and products with customers." (#N12)

In terms of learning engagement and persistence, sales employees engage with the AI coach in a highly selective, need-driven manner, guided primarily by their self-calibration goals. Their learning engagement is not measured by the volume of practice but by the strategic value of each session in advancing their professional development.

"I mainly practice when I encounter problems. Some of our more experienced, highly capable salespeople feel they don't need to practice because their experience is so rich---they're even more professional and precise than AI." (#S2)

"Through deeper usage, I've realized 'Lianlian' can scientifically and accurately guide us in evaluating our own abilities." (#S8)

Non-sales employees, by contrast, emphasize sustained engagement through habit formation. They integrate AI practice into their daily routines, leveraging the AI coach to steadily accumulate knowledge and enhance skills.

"I practice daily. Every day I can learn more. Since I'm new and not very familiar with company projects, 'Lianlian' has many projects I can learn about daily---including how products are used and how to introduce them to customers." (#N4)

"We practice whenever we have free time---it's become a habit, or rather part of our work routine." (#N11)

In terms of achievement motivation, sales employees embrace challenge-driven engagement, treating the AI coach as a formidable customer and deriving immense satisfaction from overcoming difficult modules.

"Colleagues all discuss that selling cards in 'Lianlian' is very difficult. Then I became obsessed with challenging this scenario. Later I found that once I understood its characteristics, success came easily." (#S4)

"When practicing, I try every possible way to persuade it, to challenge it, to master it." (#S8)

Meanwhile, non-sales employees enter a knowledge-acquisition feedback loop, where the unpredictability of questions yields positive reinforcement when answered correctly, driving deeper engagement.

"During practice, you don't know what problem will come next. At first I'd panic, unsure how to respond. But as I learned more, I became more familiar and fluent." (#N1)

"Initially, practicing while busy with work was exhausting. But later, correctly answering more professional questions motivated me to keep practicing." (#N9)

**Mechanism 3: Boundary spanning**

Boundary spanning captures how AI coaching expands role boundaries and fosters team alignment. By simulating cross-functional scenarios and providing access to broader knowledge systems, AI allows employees to accumulate diverse experiential knowledge, experiment with new tasks, and envision themselves in expanded roles. We found that this mechanism is especially critical for non-sales employees, since they can explore and rehearse sales interactions in a psychologically safe space, thereby transitioning into hybrid roles.

In terms of job enrichment, sales employees achieve multi-scenario experience accumulation. By virtually rehearsing different situations, they simulate on-the-job learning across diverse contexts.

"The scenarios in 'Lianlian' are very comprehensive---basically all possible customer questions can be practiced. If a customer asks something slightly different, I can adapt from similar cases I've practiced. It's like gaining work experience through practice." (#S6)

"Our company has launched many new projects. For medical areas like dentistry that we hadn't encountered before, 'Lianlian' helps us learn much faster, allowing us to recommend more projects to customers." (#S6)

Non-sales employees experience proactive sales expansion. They leverage the AI coach to achieve meaningful job enrichment by gaining firsthand exposure to sales responsibilities. Even without holding formal sales roles, they practiced sales dialogues, learned sales strategies, and simulated customer interactions, thereby building transferable skills and accumulating practical experience that broadened their professional capabilities beyond their technical duties.

"A big change is that after completing a project with a customer, I now proactively chat with them about their needs. This brings me closer to customers, building better relationships and starting to accumulate my own client base." (#N13)

"Before, if you weren't in that position, you couldn't get trained for related skills. For example, as a non-sales employee, with AI coaching, I can accumulate sales experience and develop sales skills." (#N14)

In terms of identity development, sales employees experience professional identity consolidation. They feel their role strengthened as expert advisors, experiencing greater professionalism and work vitality.

"For us in sales, professionalism is extremely important. I feel 'Lianlian' has helped me improve professionally, making me more competitive in this position." (#S6)

"I feel more energized at work because with stronger professional skills, I have more confidence." (#S7)

Non-sales employees, by contrast, experience role integration transition. Through AI-driven sales simulations, they develop a fundamentally new understanding of their professional identity. This immersive practice enabled them to blend their technical expertise with consultative skills, creating a hybrid identity that views service delivery and sales as one continuous flow.

"Currently I'm in technical position, but later I would try to do both technical and sales work---expanding more possibilities." (#N9)

"At first, we'd choose practice content based on our current positions. Later as content expanded, we started exploring and practicing other roles, especially sales." (#N21)

In terms of team collaboration, sales employees benefit from unified knowledge structure, as shared AI-based training aligns terminology and methodology across the organization.

"There's less discussion about 'Lianlian' among colleagues because the knowledge is the same, though practice methods vary by individual." (#S1)

"Previous training couldn't achieve this kind of batch learning. With 'Lianlian', we've incorporated much professional knowledge into training materials, so the entire company learns the same content, which customers can sense is unified." (#S8)

Non-sales employees experience collaborative logic alignment. They emphasized that consistent AI practice fosters tacit understanding and smoother teamwork:

"Practice can lead to more coordinated teamwork. When serving customers, we can complement each other well." (#N5, #N6, #N8)

"For example, some customers might be served by several employees simultaneously, which requires good coordination and professionalism. After practicing, we have developed tacit understanding." (#N21)

By leveraging AI coach, employees benefit from cognitive support, tap into motivational empowerment, and engage in boundary spanning to elevate their performance. Crucially, sales and non-sales employees draw on these mechanisms in distinct ways, reflecting their differing starting points and job demands. Recognizing and adapting to these nuanced variations ensures that AI coaching delivers maximum impact across the organization.

**Finding 4: The Differentiated Outcomes of AI Coaching**

Finally, we found that AI coaching produced two key outcome dimensions across all employees: performance and career planning. Meanwhile, sales employees and non-sales employees experienced these outcomes in subtly different ways (as shown in Table 4). While performance outcomes represent more immediate (short-term) effects of AI coaching, career planning reflects longer-term impacts on employees' professional trajectories and aspirations.

**Short-term Outcomes: Performance Improvements**

Performance, as a key outcome of AI coaching, can be evaluated along three dimensions---sales performance, customer satisfaction, and high-end service proficiency. While both sales and non-sales employees reported clear improvements across all three, the nature of their gains diverged. For sales employees, AI coaching tended to provide supplemental enhancements, fine-tuning existing communication skills and deepening product expertise and thereby boosting performance incrementally. In contrast, non-sales employees experienced fundamental transformations, acquiring core consultative abilities and confidence that were previously absent, which translated into more pronounced and rapid performance gains. This distinction explains why non-sales staff often exhibited stronger overall improvements, as AI coaching reshaped the very foundations of their customer-facing capabilities.

Sales employees reported that enhanced customer communication leads to higher sales, with AI coaching serving as a catalyst for more effective customer interactions:

"Since we can establish better communication with customers, we have achieved higher sales performance." (#S2)

"'Lianlian' provides helpful assistance, and everyone can see the improvement in sales performance." (#S7)

Non-sales employees likewise achieved higher sales, but their improvements stemmed from overcoming communication barriers rather than refining advanced techniques:

"Before, I didn't like talking much with customers. Even when I wanted to sell a product, I felt awkward communicating. But now through practice, my communication skills have improved, so I take more initiative in discussing projects and products with customers." (#N12)

"'Lianlian' is truly effective---after practicing, my membership card conversion rate with customers has improved." (#N22)

Customer satisfaction improvements were another short-term outcome, achieved through more professional and confident communication. Sales employees attributed higher satisfaction to expertise-driven interactions that built customer trust:

"With more solid professional knowledge, customers place higher trust in our communication---it no longer feels like just sales pitching." (#S1)

"I find the scenario of selling membership cards to new customers particularly helpful. Since new members tend to be more guarded, it's crucial to use your expertise and communication skills to make them receptive without feeling pressured." (#S3)

Non-sales employees achieved higher customer satisfaction through enhanced communication clarity and professional language:

"Not all beauticians are naturally talkative or good communicators. Through 'Lianlian', we can practice our language skills, which enhances overall customer satisfaction." (#N10)

"Customers recognize that I'm not just skilled in techniques but also professionally knowledgeable---the combination creates more than 100% value. This gives customers great satisfaction." (#N9)

Service proficiency represented another dimension of short-term impact, particularly in handling high-end services. Sales employees enhanced their ability to recommend premium offerings:

"For medical beauty service, stronger professional competence builds greater customer trust, thereby improving performance." (#S6)

"High-end medical beauty projects like gut microbiome and dental care weren't our daily focus before---we'd only discuss them when customers asked, so our knowledge was limited. Now we practice these through 'Lianlian'." (#S7)

Non-sales employees gained access to knowledge previously reserved for specialists:

"Basic projects are common knowledge for most of us. For premium services like dental and microbiome regulation, previously only experienced staff handled them when customers asked. Now everyone can learn and share these with clients." (#N18)

"A newcomer in my team recently told me she discussed microbiome with a client. Surprised, I asked how she knew about it---she said she practiced on 'Lianlian' and remembered, then shared when the client showed interest." (#N21)

**Long-term Outcomes: Career Planning and Professional Trajectories**

With respect to career planning, we observed that sales employees did not report any job transfer intentions, focusing instead on promotion ambitions. They described that professional growth leads to promotion ambition:

"Now I'm considering promotion opportunities, aspiring to become a more professional management talent." (#S4)

"After improving professionalism through practice, my career path has become clearer---I hope to develop deeper expertise in this industry." (#S6)

Non-sales employees, however, experienced more transformative career shifts, with AI coaching serving as a catalyst for role transitions:

"I'm currently considering transitioning to sales agent, as this role has become more attractive in our company." (#N12)

"Though currently a regular beautician, AI coaching has boosted my confidence and significantly improved my sales and communication skills. Therefore, I'm planning to develop toward sales agent." (#N14)

"I believe continued AI coaching will further perfect my professional knowledge and work capabilities, making promotion a realistic consideration." (#N1)

**Discussion**

Our qualitative findings provide a comprehensive understanding of how AI coaching transforms employee development and performance, offering several key insights. First, we contribute to the literature on AI in workplace learning by identifying a five-stage employee growth model that captures how employees progress from novice to expert in AI-driven learning environments. This model builds on Dreyfus's (1986) framework and shows how AI coaching's active, learner-driven nature accelerates skill acquisition and fosters reflective expertise.

Second, our identification of three differentiated mechanisms---cognitive support, motivational empowerment, and boundary spanning---reveals the pathways through which AI coaching benefits distinct employee groups. This contributes to understanding AI's contextual effectiveness in organizations, showing how the same technology can create different value for different users based on their professional contexts and developmental needs.

Third, by integrating quantitative performance outcomes with qualitative mechanism insights, we provide a holistic view of AI coaching's impact. The qualitative analysis explains the "black box" processes that drive the quantitative findings, demonstrating how AI coaching's transformative potential varies across employee types and manifests in both short-term performance gains and long-term career development.

Finally, our conceptual framework (Figure 2) synthesizes these findings into a comprehensive model of AI coaching effectiveness, illustrating the interconnected relationships between training paradigm transformation, employee growth trajectories, differentiated mechanisms, and varied outcomes. This framework serves as a roadmap for both researchers and practitioners seeking to maximize AI coaching's potential in workplace development.
